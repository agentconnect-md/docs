A Google Chat integration connects an agent to a Chat app built as a Google Workspace add-on. Google delivers the app's events over HTTPS callbacks only — there is no socket mode — so the deployment needs a public callback endpoint. AgentConnect Cloud provides one; a self-hosted deployment must have its public callback endpoint configured, as for [Linear](/connect-automate/triggers-automation/linear#before-you-connect). On a self-hosted deployment, Google Chat appears in the integration picker once the `google-chat` feature flag is on: the Helm chart keeps it off by default (`features.googleChat`); on Docker Compose, add `google-chat` to `FEATURE_FLAGS` in [`compose.env`](/self-hosting/docker-compose/configuration#compose-environment) and recreate `web`.

There are two ways to connect:

- **The deployment's app** answers your whole Google Workspace through your organization's built-in **AgentConnect** agent. You install it into your Workspace and connect it from Google Chat. It is available where the deployment offers one: on AgentConnect Cloud, or where a self-hosted administrator has configured the [deployment's Chat app](/self-hosting/provider-apps#google-chat-deployment-app).
- **Your own Chat app** connects any agent through a Chat app you create in your own Google Cloud project.

## Connect the deployment's app

<Image src={imgGoogleChatIntegration} alt="Install the deployment's Google Chat app from Google Workspace Marketplace" width={640} />

1. On your organization's built-in **AgentConnect** agent, open **Integrations → Add integration → Google Chat** and choose **Install from Google Workspace Marketplace**. Install the app into your Google Workspace; a Workspace administrator can install it for everyone in the domain.
2. In Google Chat, send the app a direct message, or add it to a space. Until your Workspace is connected, the app answers a message with a private **Configure** prompt, and being added with a **Connect** card.
3. Follow it to the console, sign in with the Google account you use in Google Chat, choose the organization, and click **Connect**. You need to be an Owner or Collaborator of that organization. If you signed in to the console another way, link that Google account on your profile first.
4. From the **Configure** prompt, Chat sends your message again, and the agent answers. The **Connect** card has no message to resend, so send the app a message or @-mention it once you are connected. From then on it answers everyone in your Workspace who messages the app or @-mentions it in a space.

A Workspace connects to one organization at a time; one already connected elsewhere is refused. Connect from a conversation in your own Workspace: a guest in another organization's space cannot connect it. Removing the Google Chat integration from the **AgentConnect** agent disconnects your Workspace, and you can connect it again the same way.

## Use your own Chat app

### Create the app

The wizard's **Before you start** step lists what you need:

- A Google Workspace account that can configure Chat apps.
- A Google Cloud project with the **Google Chat API** enabled.
- The **Cloud Resource Manager API** enabled in that project. AgentConnect reads the project's number through it.
- A service account in that same project with the **Browser** role, and a JSON key for it. Create the key in the Chat app's own project; a key from another project is refused.

Then, in the Google Cloud console, open **APIs & Services → Google Chat API → Configuration**:

1. Build the app as a **Google Workspace add-on**, the default for a new app. Convert an existing Chat app first; the conversion cannot be undone.
2. Set the app's name, avatar, and description.
3. Under **Functionality**, select **Receive 1:1 messages** and **Join spaces and group conversations**.
4. Under **Connection settings**, choose **HTTP endpoint URL**, use one URL for all triggers, and enter the value the wizard shows — the deployment's public callback origin followed by `/googlechat/events`, such as `https://relay.example.test/googlechat/events`.
5. Under **Commands**, add a slash command named `/help`, described "Show how to use this app", with command ID `1`. Any free ID works, since AgentConnect matches the name.
6. Under **Visibility**, choose the people and groups in your Workspace domain who may use the app. A private app needs no Marketplace listing.
7. Click **Save**.

### Connect it

On your agent, open **Integrations → Add integration → Google Chat**; on the built-in **AgentConnect** agent, choose **Use your own Google Chat app instead** first. Paste the service-account key JSON, and the wizard fills in the **Project ID** from it. The **Project number** is optional: AgentConnect looks it up, and checks it if you enter one. Click **Connect**.

AgentConnect validates the key with Google before saving it, and clears the key field after every attempt. A refusal names its fix: the wrong project, a key from another project, the Cloud Resource Manager API disabled, the Browser role missing, or the Chat API not configured.

After connecting, the wizard shows three states:

- **Saved** — Google accepted the key.
- **Connected** — the callback endpoint is live and the agent is online.
- **Added** — the app is in a space or a direct conversation.

Then test it: send the app a direct message, or add it to a space and mention it.

## Use it

- **Direct messages** — message the app directly; every message reaches the agent. Replies stream in and are edited into their final form.
- **Spaces** — add the app to a space and **@-mention** it. The agent replies in the same thread as the message. Google delivers a space message to the app only when it mentions the app, replies inside a thread included: a thread reply without the mention is never delivered, so the agent cannot answer it. For the same reason, a space's trigger offers **@-mention**, **By decision**, or **Off**, and no **any message** option.
- **Welcome and help** — when the app is added to a space or a direct message, it introduces itself. Type `/help` and pick it from Chat's command list to see the text commands.
- **Working on it** — when the agent has shown nothing after two seconds, the app posts "⏳ Working on it…", and the answer replaces it in place. If the agent decides not to answer, the message is withdrawn, and Google Chat shows that a message was deleted.
- **Formatting** — the agent's Markdown is rendered in Google Chat's supported subset: bold, italic, strikethrough, inline code, code blocks, lists, and links. Headings render as bold lines, images as links, and tables as monospace blocks. Long replies are split across messages.
- **Questions** — when the agent needs your input, it posts a card in the conversation: a button per option, or a short form with **Confirm**. Anyone who can see the card can answer it, and it updates to show the answer. **Dismiss** declines the question.

Not supported in this version: attachments in either direction, dialogs, group direct messages, slash commands other than `/help`, and reactions.

See [In-conversation commands](/connect-automate/integrations-overview#in-conversation-commands) for stopping, cancelling, queuing, and changing supported session settings.

## Visibility in the console

A **direct message** with the app stays private to the person who sent it. They can open it in the console after signing in with the same Google account, or linking it on their profile; nobody else can, organization Owners included. Conversations in a **space** are visible to the organization, whoever is in the space.

## Notes

- Your own Chat app binds to one agent, and a Google Cloud project holds one Chat app. Deleting the integration frees the app for reuse (**Use an existing bot** next time).
- Rotate your own app's key under **Integrations → Messaging apps → Google Chat** by pasting a new service-account key. The deployment app's key is managed in the Setup Server instead; after a restart, every connected Workspace uses the new key.
- Spaces the app belongs to appear under the integration. A direct conversation appears after its first message, named after the person.

A Microsoft Teams integration connects an agent to your Microsoft 365 organization. Teams delivers messages to bots over HTTPS only, so the deployment needs a public callback endpoint. AgentConnect Cloud has one.

There are two ways to connect. **Connect with Microsoft** gives the agent a Teams app and bot from AgentConnect, with no bot to register. **Use your own Teams bot** uses a bot your organization registers and installs itself.

## Connect with Microsoft

On your agent, open **Integrations → Add integration → Microsoft Teams** and click **Connect with Microsoft**.

<Image src={imgConnect} alt="The Add integration dialog with Microsoft Teams selected and the Connect with Microsoft button" width={640} />

Every agent appears in Teams as a bot of its own, under its name and icon.

- **The first connection** is your organization's built-in agent's. A Teams or Global administrator signs in and approves the app for the Microsoft 365 organization, and the app is added to the organization's apps.
- **Each further agent** gets a Teams app of its own. Once an administrator has connected the organization, anyone in it can connect another agent: sign in with your Microsoft 365 account, and the request waits for a Teams administrator to publish the agent's app.

<Image src={imgWaiting} alt="Waiting for approval: open the app in the Teams admin center, or copy the link for an administrator" width={480} />

A Teams administrator opens the link and chooses **Publish** in the Teams admin center. You do not need to keep the page open: AgentConnect finishes the connection once the app is published, and the agent's Teams card in the console shows the request until then, with **Check again** and **Cancel request**.

<Image src={imgPublish} alt="The agent's app in the Teams admin center, pending, with the Publish button" width={640} />

Then mention the agent in any channel. Teams adds it to that team, and it answers there. People add the app to a 1:1 chat from **Apps → Built for your org**, where their app setup policy allows it.

<Image src={imgMention} alt="The Teams mention picker listing two agents, each under its own name and icon" width={640} />

- A newly published agent can take a few hours before Teams delivers messages to it. Messages sent before then are not delivered later, so send them again once the agent answers.
- Two agents of one Microsoft 365 organization cannot share a name in Teams. Rename one, then connect again.
- A Microsoft 365 organization connects to one AgentConnect organization.

## Recover a connection held by another organization

If Teams says your Microsoft 365 organization is already connected elsewhere, click **Verify administrator access** on the connection page. Sign in as a current **Teams Administrator** or **Global Administrator** of that Microsoft 365 tenant. A Team owner or an AgentConnect Owner alone cannot recover it. Activate an eligible administrator role first if your organization uses PIM.

Check the destination AgentConnect organization, then click **Recover connection**. Verification alone changes nothing; you can cancel before confirming. The original installer does not need to participate, including if they have left your organization.

Recovery removes the previous organization's Teams connections that use the deployment app and connects your selected agent. Its agents and history stay in the original organization; connection settings are not transferred. Organization-owned bots are unaffected. If the app still shows the old agent's name or icon, use **Update in Teams** afterward.

Removing the app from a chat or Team does not release the whole tenant's connection. If administrator verification is unavailable, use **Contact support**; support verifies current tenant authority before releasing a binding. Never send passwords, tokens or client secrets.

## Use your own Teams bot [#register-your-own-bot]

<Image src={imgRegister} alt="Register your own Teams bot: the messaging endpoint, then the App (bot) ID, Directory (tenant) ID, and client secret" width={640} />

Choose **Use your own Teams bot instead** in the wizard. In the [Teams Developer Portal](https://dev.teams.microsoft.com/tools/bots), signed in to your Microsoft 365 organization:

1. Open **Bot management → New Bot**, name it, and click **Add**. The portal also creates the bot's app registration in Microsoft Entra.
2. Under **Configure**, paste the **messaging endpoint** the wizard shows as the endpoint address, and click **Save**.
3. Under **Client secrets**, click **Add a client secret for your bot** and copy the value. Teams shows it only once.
4. Copy the **Bot ID** from **Configure**.
5. In [Microsoft Entra → App registrations](https://entra.microsoft.com/#view/Microsoft_AAD_RegisteredApps/ApplicationsListBlade), open the bot's app and copy its **Directory (tenant) ID** from **Overview**.

An Azure Bot resource with the same messaging endpoint works too.

Paste the **App (bot) ID**, the **Directory (tenant) ID**, and the **client secret** into the wizard, and click **Connect**. AgentConnect checks the three together with Microsoft Entra before saving them.

Then click **Download app package** and upload it in the [Teams admin center](https://admin.teams.microsoft.com/policies/manage-apps) under **Teams apps → Manage apps → Upload new app**. Open the app and choose **Add to team**: the bot answers in that team's channels right away.

- People can then add the app to a 1:1 chat. A newly uploaded app can take a few hours to open in 1:1 chats.
- For people to upload the package themselves, allow custom apps in their app setup policy.

## Keep the app current

The agent's name and icon live in the Teams app. When they change, the bot's row under **Integrations → Messaging apps → Microsoft Teams** says that **Teams has an older version of this app**.

- With **Connect with Microsoft**, click **Update in Teams** and sign in as an administrator again. The app is updated in your organization and in the teams that already have it.
- With your own bot, click **Download app package** and upload it again in the Teams admin center. It updates the same Teams app.

## Use it

- **1:1 chats** — message the bot directly. A 1:1 chat is one ongoing session with the agent.
- **Channels** — mention the bot in a post to start a session in that post's thread; replies in the thread continue it. The channel's trigger on the agent's Integrations card decides whether an unmentioned new post starts work. A channel appears on the card after its first mention. In a private or shared channel, Teams sends the bot only messages that mention it, so mention the bot in each reply that should continue the session; there, **All messages** works like **@-mentions**.
- **Group chats** — mention the bot to start a session; reply to the agent's message to continue it. To keep one session for the whole chat, set its **Session mode** to **Single session**.
- **Replies** — the agent posts its answer once and edits it in place while it works, with Markdown formatting.
- **Questions and approvals** — the agent sends a card with a button per option. Tap your answer, and the card updates for everyone in the conversation. Who may answer an approval is set by the agent's [**What people can do in chat**](/build-your-team/agents/configure-an-agent#what-people-can-do-in-chat).

See [In-conversation commands](/connect-automate/integrations-overview#in-conversation-commands) for stopping, cancelling, queuing, and changing supported session settings.

## Channels shared with other organizations

A shared channel that includes people from another organization, or a conversation a guest joins, is an external place. It starts **Off** for the agent, even where new conversations are otherwise on. A mention there gets a one-time note that the agent isn't enabled; an editor turns the channel on from the agent's Integrations card.

Once it is on, the agent answers there, but an approval it needs is not posted in the channel. The person who asked gets the approval card in their own 1:1 chat with the bot, when they belong to your organization. Otherwise one Agent editor who linked their Microsoft account gets it in their 1:1 chat with the bot, chosen as [Sessions](/operate-govern/sessions#when-a-run-asks-for-permission) describes; any editor can also answer it in the console.

## Follow Teams access

An Owner can make Teams sessions visible only to the people in each conversation, under **Settings → Session access → Require Microsoft Teams access**. Each person links their Microsoft work or school account under **Profile → Sign-in methods**; the conversation's bot then checks that they are a current member of the 1:1 chat, group chat, or channel. Guests from other organizations cannot be matched, so they do not see synced sessions. With this on, only the agent's editors may answer approvals and change runtime settings from Teams, and an approval in a channel shared with another organization goes to an editor's 1:1 chat instead of the asker's. See [Session visibility](/operate-govern/permissions-overview/session-visibility#session-access).

## Notes

- Files work in 1:1 chats. Attach a file for the agent to read, or ask it to share one: Teams asks you to allow the file, then saves it to your OneDrive and shows it in the chat. Allowing a file needs the app added for you, from **Apps → Built for your org**. Files in channels and group chats are not supported yet.
- One Teams bot binds to one agent. Deleting the integration frees your own bot for reuse (**Use an existing bot** next time); it does not delete the bot or its app registration.
- Replace your own bot's client secret under **Integrations → Messaging apps → Microsoft Teams**. The bot's conversations are unchanged. The deployment app's secret is managed in the [Setup Server](/self-hosting/provider-apps#microsoft-teams-deployment-app).

## Self-hosting

A self-hosted deployment needs its public callback endpoint configured, as for [Linear](/connect-automate/triggers-automation/linear#before-you-connect). **Connect with Microsoft** appears once an operator configures the [Microsoft Teams deployment app](/self-hosting/provider-apps#microsoft-teams-deployment-app). Without [agent bots](/self-hosting/provider-apps#agent-bots), that app serves one agent per Microsoft 365 organization, and further agents use their own Teams bots.

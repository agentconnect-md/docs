An organization can have its people sign in through its own identity provider. Three things build on each other, and you can stop after any of them:

| Step | What it changes |
| --- | --- |
| [Verify a domain](#verify-a-domain) | Proves the organization controls an email domain. Nothing else changes yet. |
| [Activate a connection](#activate) | Addresses on your verified domains can sign in only through your identity provider. |
| [Enforce](#enforce-single-sign-on) | Every member, Owners included, reaches the organization only from an SSO session. |

Who becomes a member is a separate choice, [New members](#new-members), and works with or without a connection.

Owners set all of this up under **Settings → Single sign-on**. Other members see the tab once there is a domain or a connection to read, and cannot change it.

## Verify a domain

1. In **Domains**, choose **Add domain** and enter the domain, such as `acme.example`.
2. At your DNS provider, create the TXT record the row shows:

   | Type | Name | Value |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | TXT | `_agentconnect-challenge.acme.example` | `agentconnect-verification=…` |

3. Choose **Verify**. The row changes from **Pending** to **Verified**.

**Record not found** right after you create the record usually means DNS has not caught up; wait and choose **Verify** again. A pending domain that is not verified within seven days is dropped, and you can add it again.

- A domain belongs to one organization. If another organization verifies it first, your row shows **Verified by another organization**.
- A domain is matched exactly. Add `eu.acme.example` as its own domain if people have addresses there.
- An organization can hold up to 20 domains.
- Keep the TXT record in place. AgentConnect looks it up again every day and marks the row **DNS record missing** when it is gone, and activating a connection is refused until it is back.

## Set up the connection

Once a domain is verified, **Connection** shows **Set up connection**. Choose the protocol your identity provider uses. The dialog shows the values to register there before you save anything, so you can create the application at the provider first and come back with what it issues.

**OIDC**

| Field | Enter |
| --- | --- |
| **Issuer** | The provider's issuer URL |
| **Client ID**, **Client secret** | The credentials the provider issues for the application |
| **Scopes** | `openid email profile` unless the provider needs more to return an address |
| **Callback URL** | Shown for you to copy: register it at the provider as the redirect URI |

**SAML**

| Field | Enter |
| --- | --- |
| **Metadata** | The provider's metadata URL, or its metadata XML |
| **Email attribute** | The attribute that carries the address; `email` by default |
| **Name attribute** | The attribute that carries the display name; `name` by default |
| **ACS URL**, **Entity ID** | Shown for you to copy: register them at the provider |

The protocol is fixed once the connection exists. To switch, remove the connection and set one up again, which gives new values to register.

A connection speaks only for addresses on your verified domains. A sign-in that returns no address, or one on another domain, is refused. AgentConnect takes the address as your provider sends it, so use a provider where people cannot set their own.

## Test the sign-in

A new connection reads **Not tested**. Choose **Test sign-in**: you sign in once through your identity provider and land on a result page.

- **Sign-in test passed** shows the address that came back. Choose **Back to Settings**; the connection now reads **Tested**.
- **Sign-in test failed** says what was missing. When the provider returned no address on your domains, the page lists **Attributes the provider sent**, by name only, and marks the ones that hold an **Address in your domains**. Set one of those as the **Email attribute** (or fix the email claim for OIDC) and test again.

Two things to know before you start a test:

- For ten minutes after it starts, addresses on your domains can sign in only through the connection, exactly as when it is active.
- If the provider returns someone else's address, you are signed in as that account and need to sign back in as yourself.

Changing the connection's settings later sets it back to **Not tested** until a test passes again.

## Activate

**Activate** is offered once a domain is verified and the connection is tested. From that moment:

- addresses on your verified domains can sign in only with SSO, and other sign-in methods are refused for them; each domain row shows **SSO sign-in only**;
- a person who already has an account under such an address keeps it: their first SSO sign-in reaches the same account, memberships and settings;
- a domain you verify afterwards is covered as soon as it is verified, after a confirmation.

Activation changes how those addresses sign in. It does not add or remove any member.

## How members sign in

On the sign-in page, members choose **Continue with SSO**, enter their **Work email**, and are sent to your identity provider. The SSO page has its own address, `/login/sso` on the console, so people who always sign in this way can bookmark it and skip the first page.

The sign-in page marks the method a browser used last with **Last used**, and the SSO page fills in the address used last. An address whose domain has no connection gets **No single sign-on is set up for this email.**

## New members

**Settings → Members → New members** appears once a domain is verified:

| Choice | Who joins |
| --- | --- |
| **Invite only** (default) | Only people an Owner adds or who open an invite link |
| **Anyone on your domains** | Also anyone who signs in with an address on a verified domain |

A person who joins by domain becomes a **Collaborator**, once:

- someone an Owner later removes is not added back at their next sign-in; invite them to bring them back;
- someone who is already a member keeps their role;
- someone who was signed in when you made the choice joins at their next sign-in.

This choice does not need a connection. With a verified domain alone, people on it join when they sign in by any method. Removing your last verified domain returns the organization to **Invite only**.

The address is the one on the person's AgentConnect account. Someone who has left your company but can still sign in to that account another way still counts as on your domain. Remove them from **Members**, and [enforce single sign-on](#enforce-single-sign-on) when you want your identity provider to decide who gets in.

## Enforce single sign-on

An active connection restricts how your domains' addresses sign in, but an account can hold several sign-in methods. Enforcing closes that gap: every member, Owners included, opens the organization only from a browser session that signed in through the connection.

**Enforce** is on an **Active** connection. You can enforce when you are an Owner whose own address is on the verified domains and your current session signed in with SSO. If it did not, the dialog offers **Sign in with SSO** and brings you back.

While the connection reads **Enforced**:

- a member who signed in another way sees **_Acme_ requires single sign-on** in place of the organization, with **Sign in with SSO**. Their other organizations are unaffected.
- a member whose address is not on your verified domains cannot sign in with SSO and loses access. Give contractors an address on your domain at the identity provider, or do not enforce.
- creating a personal API key or approving an OAuth grant for the organization also needs an SSO session. Keys, OAuth tokens and service accounts issued earlier keep working, so revoke the ones a departing person held.

**Stop enforcing** returns the organization to any sign-in; the connection stays active. Editing the connection's settings or deactivating it also ends enforcement, and it returns only when an Owner enforces again.

If your identity provider breaks while SSO is enforced and no Owner can sign in, a deployment administrator can turn enforcement off.

## Change or remove

| Action | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| **Edit** | New settings apply at once. The connection stays active, reads **Not tested** until a test passes, and is no longer enforced. For OIDC the stored client secret is kept unless you choose **Replace** or change the issuer. |
| **Deactivate** | Addresses on your domains can sign in with any method again. People who signed in through the connection can still use it. |
| **Remove connection** (in **Edit**) | The connection and the SSO sign-ins it set up are deleted. Accounts stay, and their owners sign in with another method. |
| **Remove domain** | A pending domain can be removed at any time; a verified one only while the organization has no connection. |

A connection that reads **Needs configuration** has lost its settings at the sign-in service. Choose **Reconfigure** and enter them again; the values to register may have changed.

## On your profile

**Your profile → Sign-in methods** lists the identity an organization's connection gave you as **SSO**, with its address. It cannot be unlinked there; it goes away when the organization removes its connection. See [Linked accounts](/how-to-guides/linked-accounts) for the other methods.

## Self-hosting

Everything above exists on a deployment whose operator lets organizations set up their own SSO. A deployment that belongs to one company more often uses a single connector for the whole deployment, configured by the operator, with its own **New members** choice. [Sign-in](/self-hosting/sign-in#single-sign-on) covers both.
